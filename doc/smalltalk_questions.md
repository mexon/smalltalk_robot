# Smalltalk dialogue for museum robot

## Criteria Matthew

The following questions are designed to satisfy the following criteria:

1. Short exchanges, suitable for the short period between museum exhibits
2. Allow for yes/no or monosyllabic answers as well as longer answers
3. Conform to usual social expectations between strangers: no probing personal questions
4. Do not require additional knowledge about the museum visitor
5. Lead to responses that can be analysed for openness and friendliness 
6. Should be independent of each other

## Analysis of Answers

We would expect to judge the comfort level of visitors by the length
of the responses, much more than the content.  For example, when asked
if we should hurry up, an uncomfortable visitor might simply say "it's
fine", while another visitor might say that they have half an hour
before lunch, but that should be enough.

We do not expect to incorporate the answers into later responses or
later questions.  This would be a good way to improve the visitor's
experience with the robot, but our research concerns using small talk
responses to assess the visitor's mood and respond in other ways.  If
we attempt to manipulate that mood, it may confound the results.  This
is one reason the questions should be chosen to be independent of one
another.  It also reduces cognitive load for the visitor, who does not
have to balance two conversational threads simultaneousl.  Finally, it
also reduces the complexity of the LLM prompt.
